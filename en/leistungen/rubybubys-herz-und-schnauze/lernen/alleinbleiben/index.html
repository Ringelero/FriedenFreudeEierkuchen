Preparation differs from treating fear

For a relaxed dog, begin with brief distances within the home. A resting place and a suitable familiar activity can help. Do not start with a long absence to see whether they get used to it.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Time alone

Observe the time without you

A recording can reveal what you no longer see on returning. Notice whether your dog rests comfortably or searches, paces, pants or vocalises. Silence alone does not prove relaxation. Adjust the next attempt to the earliest sign of difficulty.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Separation anxiety

If separation is already distressing

Arrange trusted care for unavoidable longer absences for now. Do not wait out ongoing distress or punish damage and accidents. An individual behaviour plan with veterinary involvement is more appropriate than a universal weekly schedule.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Separation anxiety

Our suggested agreement

Write down which brief separation is manageable and who can help for now. A shared plan prevents different people from setting a bigger test every day.
