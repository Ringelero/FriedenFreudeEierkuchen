Out together

Give dog encounters more space

Choose distance, keep an exit and allow contact to be optional.

In this guide

Decide before the encounter

A narrow pavement is a poor place to force contact. Cross early when safe, take a curve or use an open turning. For a planned walk, begin in the same direction with enough space for both dogs to turn their attention back to the surroundings.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Reactive dogs · Battersea · Introducing dogs on walks

Contact is an option

Ask the other person before allowing an approach. Brief contact may be enough; moving on remains an option. Tangled, tight leads and forced head-on meetings make the situation harder. Both people need to support an exit.

Read more: Battersea · Introducing dogs on walks

If your dog is already barking or lunging

First create distance calmly rather than demanding obedience beside the trigger. A familiar turn-away cue can help earlier; new learning belongs in an easier setting. Repeated intense reactions or sudden changes need individual assessment.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Reactive dogs

A useful note after the walk

Note which distance and path width helped. “Parallel walking on the wide path worked; the gate was too tight” helps the next plan more than a broad label such as “unsociable”.
