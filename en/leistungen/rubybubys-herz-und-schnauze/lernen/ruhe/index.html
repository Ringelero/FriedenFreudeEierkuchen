Puppies & everyday life

Make rest possible rather than holding it in place

Build a suitable resting place and relaxed moments.

Check the setting first

Is the spot comfortable and away from constant foot traffic and draughts? Have movement, water, food and a toilet break been considered? A dog watching every passer-by at the window may need a different resting place.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Restlessness

A quiet exercise beside you

Sit in a low-distraction place first. A blanket can mark the spot. Reward quiet behaviour calmly and close to the resting place. Do not insist on perfect lying down: briefly looking away from activity may be a suitable start. Later, a looser body and resting the head matter more than simply remaining still.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Settling

If the reward makes everything exciting again

Our adjustment: try smaller rewards delivered more quietly and check whether a calmer location helps. Do not force sleep through restraint or an ever longer activity marathon. Protect actual sleep from interruptions.

Investigate ongoing restlessness

Sudden new or persistent intense restlessness can be more than a training issue. Record the setting, daily routine and changes for your vet.
