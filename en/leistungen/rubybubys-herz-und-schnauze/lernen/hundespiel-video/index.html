Who adapts during play?

Watch the whole clip first. Then compare both dogs’ movements and open each explanation after answering for yourself.

Observe first, then compare.

Explanations stay closed until you open them. There are no scores or time limits.

01What are the two dogs doing with the ring?

An observable description would be: both hold the ring with their mouths while the smaller dog changes position. “The bigger one lets them win” already includes interpretation.

02Who changes position more often in roughly the first 14 seconds?

The larger brown dog mostly stays in the same place, moving particularly their head. The smaller black-and-white dog repeatedly changes sides around the ring. Compare the sequence rather than one still image.

03What might support an interpretation of adaptation, and what remains uncertain?

The larger dog not following every position change with their whole body fits the article’s interpretation. Their intention is not directly visible. Age, history and health cannot reliably be determined from the short clip.

04Does the camera pan at about 14–17 seconds show a play break?

We do not know. The original pair is temporarily not fully visible; another dog and part of a person appear. Missing coverage does not prove a break. The pair are later visible with the ring again.

05Does the clip prove both could stop whenever they wanted?

It does not show a clear, fully traceable sequence of one dog trying to leave and the other allowing it. It is useful for comparing movement, but alone does not establish that every interaction is comfortable or voluntary throughout.

06Your dog moves behind you and the other keeps following. What do you do?

Ask the other person to take their dog out of contact and create distance. Your dog can stay beside you or move on. You do not need an exact emotional diagnosis to support visible withdrawal.

Use this at your next meeting

At your next supervised meeting, note an action and response: “A turned away; B stopped.” Do not alter a situation to create a test. The ring is not a recommendation to introduce unfamiliar dogs through a shared toy.
