When things get difficult

When your dog guards food or objects

Create space and safety before working on change.

In this guide

What matters right now

If your dog freezes over a bowl or growls as you approach, create distance. Allow undisturbed eating and keep people and animals away, for example using a door or gate. Do not put your hand in the bowl to make your dog get used to it.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Resource guarding

A trading game is not a universal treatment

Repeated removal can increase concern about losing an item. Do not chase your dog or force their mouth open. Dangerous swallowed items or immediate injury risk need veterinary or professional help. Repeated guarding requires an individually safe plan.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Resource guarding

Prepare for support

Note the item, location, distance and what happened immediately beforehand. Do not create a risky situation for a video. Ask for a reward-based approach that protects people and the dog and considers possible pain.

Read more: AVSAB · Humane training & choosing a trainer

For everyday life together

Our organisational suggestion: all carers share the same rule for undisturbed meals. Offer valuable chews where there is no competition. This prevents avoidable conflict while you arrange support.
