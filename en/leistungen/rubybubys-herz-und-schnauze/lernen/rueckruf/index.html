Start where your dog can notice you

In a quiet room, a few steps are enough. Agree on one recall word for everyone to use. When your dog is attentive, say it warmly, move a little away and reward arriving with you. Choose something they actually value.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Recall

Change one difficulty outdoors

  1. Practise in a quiet, secure place first. Outside enclosed spaces, a safely handled long line attached to a harness helps manage risk.
  2. Increase either distance or a mild distraction first, rather than both together.
  3. Call while returning is realistic. Welcome your dog warmly even if they take time.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Recall

If you get no response

Do not repeat the word ever louder. Manage the situation and use a shorter distance or quieter place for the next attempt. Do not start learning in the middle of intense play. Off-lead access needs suitable local rules and a genuinely safe setting.

Our practice note: “A few steps in the courtyard work; a visible ball is too much.” The next attempt starts much farther from the ball. This keeps the task specific.
