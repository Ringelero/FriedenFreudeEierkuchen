Check paws after a walk

Notice dirt, changes and persistent licking.

A short voluntary check

Let your dog offer a paw comfortably or support it gently. Look at pads, nails and accessible spaces between toes. Notice new redness, swelling, a crack or something visibly attached. Do not force the toes apart.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Paw licking · PDSA · Grooming

Remove dirt without concealing problems

Gently rinse superficial dirt with water and dry afterwards, especially between toes. Do not dig into painful areas or try to operate on an embedded foreign body. Limping, a wound or substantial swelling need veterinary care.

If licking continues

Persistent licking and reddish-brown staining are not simply a cleanliness issue. Investigate the cause rather than only wiping away staining or applying a product by guesswork.

Read more: PDSA · Excessive licking

A useful observation

Our suggested note: which paw, since when, after which route and with what change? These details are more useful at an appointment than simply saying “licks a lot”.
