When a puppy grabs hands and clothes

Redirect early, reduce the chase game and notice the need for rest.

Prepare for the familiar difficult moment

If it regularly happens during evening play, have an appropriate toy ready beforehand. Offer it before hands or trouser legs become interesting. Chews and toys need suitable size, material and supervision.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Puppy biting

What to do in the moment

  1. Stop rapid hand and foot movements where safe. Pulling away can keep the game going.
  2. Use a brief pause to offer a suitable toy. Keep your fingers out of the game.
  3. If it stays too intense, create a short break, for example using a prepared baby gate. Follow with a calmer suitable activity.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Puppy biting · Battersea · Puppy mouthing

More activity is not always the answer

Also check sleep, a toilet break and the length of the last activity. An overtired puppy can look especially energetic. Our note-taking question: did it follow a long visit or a missed rest?

Read more: Battersea · Puppy mouthing

If it feels different from ordinary puppy play

With injuries, stiff defensive behaviour or a sudden change in biting, do not simply continue practising. Create safety and seek individual support; pain or fear may be involved.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Puppy biting
