Care & hygiene

Build up tooth brushing gradually

From lip contact to brushing without forcing the mouth open.

In this guide

Materials and starting point

Use a suitable soft dog toothbrush and dog-safe toothpaste. Human toothpaste is unsuitable. Have pain, bleeding or substantial tartar examined first; harder scrubbing does not replace treatment.

Read more: PDSA · Dental care · PDSA · Plaque and brushing

Touch before brushing

  1. Let your dog taste a little toothpaste. Separately practise a brief touch to the outside of the lip.
  2. When lifting the lip is comfortable, reward and pause again. There is no need to force the mouth wide open.
  3. Build up a brief brush contact on the outer tooth surfaces. Extend only when your dog is comfortable.

Read more: PDSA · Checking your dog’s mouth · PDSA · Dental care

A routine rather than a struggle

Aim for regular, ideally daily brushing. Build this over small sessions rather than attempting every tooth immediately. Ask your clinic to demonstrate brush movement and difficult areas. Dental chews can supplement brushing but do not replace it.

Read more: PDSA · Dental care · PDSA · Plaque and brushing

Our suggested repeat

Note “lifting the lip works; the brush is still difficult”. Repeat the comfortable contact and make the brush step easier next time. Continuing to eat does not prove a dog has no dental pain.
