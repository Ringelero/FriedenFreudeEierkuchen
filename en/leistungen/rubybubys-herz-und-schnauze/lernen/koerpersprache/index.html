What is my dog telling me?

Read signals in context instead of judging feelings from a wagging tail.

Describe the change first

At a café, your dog turns their head as someone reaches towards them. Compare it with the moment before: does their body stiffen? Do they remain close willingly or move back? A single yawn or wag does not explain the situation.

Read more: Dogs Trust · Body language

A short observation exercise

  1. Note the trigger: who approaches, touches or moves?
  2. Describe visible movements and distance without treating a feeling as a fact.
  3. Reduce pressure: the person withdraws their hand or you move further apart. Notice what changes.

Our example of a useful note: “When the person leaned over, he became still and stepped back; with more space, he started sniffing again.” This suggests a practical change: give the next greeting more room.

When to create space immediately

Freezing, continued avoidance, growling or snapping are reasons to stop approaching. Do not test how much your dog will tolerate. Repeated or suddenly new reactions need professional assessment.
