Four areas connecting animals and people.

01Meetups & walksSee friends againMeeting points & registration available+

Shared walks and small meetups aim to help familiar relationships grow. Save your favourite meeting points and see who's out and about. You don't need to belong to a particular neighbourhood.

Open meeting points & walking windows

How we imagine meetups

  • A clear meeting point, a time and a contact person you can reach.
  • Suitable encounters with room for distance, rest and retreat.
  • Clear agreements about who's joining and how the walk will go.

Drop by without committing

Dog walks have flexible two-hour windows. You can let others know you're coming with a dog or as a companion without one, and tap “I'm here now” when you arrive.

Special meetups may have their own arrangements. Changes and cancellations appear directly on the meetup.

Planned events

For a casual dog walk, go straight to the meeting points with two-hour windows. Additional planned events with their own registration are listed here.

02Learning togetherUnderstanding, trust, freedomThree small starting points+

We want to get to know dogs, take their needs seriously and learn together. Attention, clear signals and reward-based learning are our starting point.

Explore the learning area ↗

Observe and understand

How does the animal experience a situation? Learn about body language and the specific context before setting expectations for behaviour.

Learning through reinforcement

Support desired behaviour with suitable rewards. The veterinary behaviour society AVSAB recommends reward-based methods.

AVSAB: dog training recommendations

A quiet place

Our meetups should allow distance and retreat. We adapt their frequency and length to each individual animal.

Our approach and the foundations of learning

Krishnamurti's thoughts on relationships and freedom inspire us to observe and understand attentively. Applying them to life with animals is our own interpretation. Practical dog exercises are developed using animal behaviour sources.

Krishnamurti Foundation: on relationships · AVSAB: professional foundations

In the learning area you'll find initial ideas on body language, reward-based learning and rest. Each starting point includes a small practical step and the relevant expert sources.

03Dog walks & careSharing careDog walks & care+

People who live with an animal should be able to find support in daily life. Neighbours without a dog of their own can also join walks and gradually become trusted people.

Growing familiar, step by step

  1. Get to know each other at a meetup.
  2. Walk together and discuss needs.
  3. Arrange suitable individual walking or care times.

Find or offer dog-walking help

You can join a walk even without a dog of your own. For individual dog walks, you can create a listing, express interest privately and take time to get to know each other.

Go to dog-walking help

Day care, overnight stays and several days are possible too. Under My contacts & care arrangements you can save trusted people and privately agree on exact dates and times.

04Animal-friendly placesMore room to livePrepare for first conversations+

Restaurants, hostels and other businesses should be able to join the network. Suitable opportunities for meeting, spending time and resting give animals more familiar places.

In preparation

A growing network of hosts

First, we'll get to know restaurants, hostels and other places. Together, we'll clarify which animals can be welcome, when a visit would work and what the place can offer. No business has been listed as a participating host yet.

On the host page, you can explore the idea and prepare conversation notes. A public listing will follow later, after a mutual agreement.
